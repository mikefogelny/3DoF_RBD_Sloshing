\section{Spacecraft Attitude Dynamics and Control Model}
\label{sec:attitude-model}

This section summarises the equations of motion used in the closed-loop
attitude simulation. Sign conventions and equation numbering follow
Markley and Crassidis~\cite{markley2014} throughout.


% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Quaternion Convention}
\label{sec:quat-convention}

We adopt the \emph{scalar-last} unit quaternion
\begin{equation}
\bm{q} \;=\; \begin{bmatrix} \bm{q}_v \\ q_4 \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{4},
\qquad \bm{q}_v \in \mathbb{R}^{3},\; q_4 \in \mathbb{R},\; \|\bm{q}\| = 1,
\label{eq:scalar-last-q}
\end{equation}
matching the layout used in Eq.~(2.84) of~\cite{markley2014}. The
quaternion product is defined such that
$\bm{A}(\bm{q}\otimes\bm{p}) = \bm{A}(\bm{q})\,\bm{A}(\bm{p})$
(Eq.~2.82b of~\cite{markley2014}):
\begin{equation}
\bm{q}\otimes\bm{p} \;=\;
\begin{bmatrix}
 q_4\,\bm{p}_v + p_4\,\bm{q}_v - \bm{q}_v\times\bm{p}_v \\[2pt]
 q_4\,p_4 - \bm{q}_v^{\!\top}\bm{p}_v
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\bm{q}^{-1} \;=\; \begin{bmatrix} -\bm{q}_v \\ q_4 \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}


% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Quaternion Kinematics}
\label{sec:quat-kinematics}

The rate of change of the body-to-inertial quaternion under body angular
velocity $\bm{\omega}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$ is
(Eq.~2.88 of~\cite{markley2014})
\begin{equation}
\dot{\bm{q}}
\;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,\bm{\Omega}(\bm{\omega})\,\bm{q}
\;=\;
\begin{bmatrix}
 \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(q_4\,\bm{\omega} - \bm{\omega}\times\bm{q}_v\bigr) \\[3pt]
-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\bm{\omega}^{\!\top}\bm{q}_v
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:quat-kin}
\end{equation}


% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Rigid-Body Dynamics with Reaction Wheels}
\label{sec:euler-wheels}

For a spacecraft with symmetric, positive-definite inertia tensor
$\bm{J}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times3}$ (Eq.~3.13 of~\cite{markley2014})
and internal wheel angular momentum $\bm{h}_w^{\,b}$ expressed in the body
frame, Euler's rotational equation generalises to (Sec.~7.2
of~\cite{markley2014})
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
\bm{J}\,\dot{\bm{\omega}}
\;=\;
\bm{u}_{\text{body}}
\;-\;
\bm{\omega}\times\!\bigl(\bm{J}\bm{\omega} + \bm{h}_w^{\,b}\bigr).
\;}
\label{eq:euler-wheels}
\end{equation}
The total external torque on the body collects three contributions:
\begin{equation}
\bm{u}_{\text{body}} \;=\; -\bm{W}\bm{\tau}_w \;+\; \bm{\tau}_{\text{mag}}
\;+\; \bm{T}_{\!s},
\label{eq:u-body-decomp}
\end{equation}
the wheel reaction torque, the magnetic-torquer torque, and the propellant
slosh torque defined in Sections~\ref{sec:bdot} and~\ref{sec:slosh}. A full
breakdown of these three contributions — including which is exported as
``control torque'' in the MATLAB data files and which is not — is given in
Section~\ref{sec:u-body-detail}.


% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Reaction Wheels and Torque Allocation}
\label{sec:wheels}

We model $n_w$ momentum wheels whose spin-axis unit vectors form the
columns of a configuration matrix
$\bm{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times n_w}$. The scalar wheel momenta about
their spin axes are stacked into $\bm{h}_w\in\mathbb{R}^{n_w}$, so the
body-frame wheel momentum is
\begin{equation}
\bm{h}_w^{\,b} \;=\; \bm{W}\,\bm{h}_w.
\end{equation}
With wheel torque commands $\bm{\tau}_w\in\mathbb{R}^{n_w}$, the wheels
react $-\bm{W}\bm{\tau}_w$ onto the body and their internal momentum
evolves as
\begin{equation}
\dot{\bm{h}}_w \;=\; \bm{\tau}_w.
\end{equation}
A desired body control torque $\bm{u}_{\text{cmd}}$ from the attitude
controller is mapped to wheel commands by the minimum-norm pseudoinverse
allocation
\begin{equation}
\bm{\tau}_w \;=\; -\bm{W}^{+}\,\bm{u}_{\text{cmd}},
\qquad
\bm{W}^{+} \;=\; \bm{W}^{\!\top}\!\bigl(\bm{W}\bm{W}^{\!\top}\bigr)^{-1}.
\label{eq:wheel-alloc}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{NASA standard four-wheel pyramid.}
The simulation uses the symmetric four-wheel pyramid configuration
(Sec.~7.2 of~\cite{markley2014}). Each spin axis is tilted by
$\beta = \arccos(1/\sqrt{3}) \approx 54.7356^\circ$ from the body $+z$
axis and the four wheels are spaced $90^\circ$ apart in azimuth. This
gives equal projection onto all three body axes and provides
single-fault tolerance.

\subsubsection{Reaction-Wheel Actuator Dynamics (Optional)}
\label{sec:wheel-actuator}

By default, each wheel is treated as an \emph{ideal} actuator: the
allocated torque command $\bm{\tau}_{w,\text{cmd}}$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:wheel-alloc})
is delivered to the body instantaneously. Real wheels cannot do this ---
motor windings and rotor inertia impose a finite spin-up/spin-down
response. This is optionally modelled (toggle \texttt{enable\_wheel\_dynamics},
default off) as a per-wheel low-pass transfer function between the
commanded and actually-delivered torque:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\tau_{w}(s)}{\tau_{w,\text{cmd}}(s)}
\;=\;
\frac{\text{num}(s)}{\text{den}(s)},
\label{eq:wheel-tf}
\end{equation}
with $\text{num}(s)$, $\text{den}(s)$ given as plain coefficient lists
(descending powers of $s$) in \texttt{SimConfig.wheel\_tf\_num} /
\texttt{wheel\_tf\_den}. The illustrative default is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\tau_w(s)}{\tau_{w,\text{cmd}}(s)}
\;=\;
\frac{1.2\,s + 0.76}{s^2 + 2.4\,s + 0.76},
\label{eq:wheel-tf-example}
\end{equation}
a second-order low-pass response. Any transfer function of any order can
be substituted by editing only these two coefficient lists: the
simulation converts $\text{num}(s)/\text{den}(s)$ to a state-space
realisation $(\bm{A}, \bm{B}, \bm{C})$ automatically via a controllable
canonical-form construction (equivalent to \texttt{scipy.signal.tf2ss}),
so no manual re-derivation is required when the actuator model changes.
Internally, this adds one extra filter state per wheel per transfer-function
pole to the simulation state vector (Sec.~\ref{sec:state}); when the toggle
is off, these states are simply absent ($\tau_w = \tau_{w,\text{cmd}}$,
recovering the original instantaneous-actuator behaviour exactly).


% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Quaternion-Feedback PD Controller}
\label{sec:pd-controller}

Given a desired attitude quaternion $\bm{q}_{\text{des}}$, the attitude
error quaternion is
\begin{equation}
\delta\bm{q} \;=\; \bm{q}_{\text{des}}^{\,-1} \otimes \bm{q}
\;=\; \bigl[\,\delta\bm{q}_v;\; \delta q_4\,\bigr]^{\!\top}.
\end{equation}
The quaternion-feedback proportional--derivative law of Sec.~7.4
of~\cite{markley2014} is used, with element-wise saturation:
\begin{equation}
\bm{u}_{\text{cmd}} \;=\;
\operatorname{sat}_{u_{\max}}\!\Bigl(
 -K_p\,\operatorname{sign}(\delta q_4)\,\delta\bm{q}_v
 \;-\; K_d\,\bm{\omega}
\Bigr),
\label{eq:pd}
\end{equation}
where $K_p, K_d > 0$ are scalar gains and
$\operatorname{sat}_{u_{\max}}(\cdot)$ clips each component to
$[-u_{\max},\, +u_{\max}]$. The factor
$\operatorname{sign}(\delta q_4)$ selects the shortest rotation and
prevents the well-known \emph{unwinding} phenomenon.


% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Magnetic Momentum Unloading}
\label{sec:bdot}

Reaction wheels accumulate angular momentum from disturbance torques
until they saturate. Magnetic torquers transfer that momentum from the
wheels to the geomagnetic field (Sec.~7.5 of~\cite{markley2014}). A
magnetic dipole moment $\bm{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$ commanded in a field
$\bm{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$ produces a body torque
\begin{equation}
\bm{\tau}_{\text{mag}} \;=\; \bm{m}\times\bm{B}.
\end{equation}
We use the cross-product (``$\bm{B}$-dot-style'') unloading law
\begin{equation}
\bm{m} \;=\;
\operatorname{sat}_{m_{\max}}\!\biggl(
\frac{K_{\text{dump}}}{\|\bm{B}\|^{2}}\,
 \bm{h}_w^{\,b} \times \bm{B}
\biggr),
\label{eq:bdot}
\end{equation}
which (before saturation) yields the body torque
\begin{equation}
\bm{\tau}_{\text{mag}}
\;=\;
-K_{\text{dump}}\,
\Bigl(\bm{h}_w^{\,b} - (\bm{h}_w^{\,b}\!\cdot\!\hat{\bm{B}})\,\hat{\bm{B}}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
i.e.\ a damping torque applied to the component of $\bm{h}_w^{\,b}$
\emph{perpendicular} to $\bm{B}$. Orbital variation of $\bm{B}$ rotates
the unloadable subspace and sweeps it through all three body axes over
each orbit. The commanded dipole is saturated element-wise to
$\bm{m}\in[-m_{\max},\,+m_{\max}]^{3}$.

For the geomagnetic field, the simulation uses a simple inclined rotating
dipole of magnitude $\sim\!30\,\mu\mathrm{T}$ as a low-Earth-orbit proxy;
in flight-grade work this is replaced by an IGRF model rotated into the
body frame.


% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Propellant Slosh Torque}
\label{sec:slosh}

Liquid propellant slosh is modelled as a forced, damped second-order
oscillator acting on a 3-D torque state $\bm{T}_{\!s}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$,
driven by body rate $\bm{\omega}$ and body angular acceleration
$\dot{\bm{\omega}}$. Two interchangeable forms of this oscillator are
implemented in the code; exactly one is active per simulation run,
selected by the configuration field \texttt{slosh\_model} (``bourdelle''
or ``testing''). Both enter Euler's equation~\eqref{eq:euler-wheels} in
exactly the same way, through $\bm{T}_{\!s}$
in~\eqref{eq:u-body-decomp}: because $\bm{T}_{\!s}$ is a \emph{state} (not
an instantaneous algebraic function of $\dot{\bm{\omega}}$), there is no
implicit loop in either case --- $\dot{\bm{\omega}}$ is computed
from~\eqref{eq:euler-wheels} using the current $\bm{T}_{\!s}$, and the
just-computed $\dot{\bm{\omega}}$ is then substituted into whichever
second-order slosh law is active.

\subsubsection{Model ``bourdelle'' (default): Time-Varying Linear Model}
\label{sec:slosh-bourdelle}

The original slosh model takes the form
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
\ddot{\bm{T}}_{\!s}
\;=\;
 -A_s(t)\,\bm{\omega}
 \;-\; B_s(t)\,\dot{\bm{\omega}}
 \;-\; C_s(t)\,\dot{\bm{T}}_{\!s}
 \;-\; K_s(t)\,\bm{T}_{\!s}.
\;}
\label{eq:slosh}
\end{equation}
The coefficients $A_s, B_s, C_s, K_s$ are scalars (identical on all three
axes) and admit an optional sinusoidal time variation about a positive
DC offset:
\begin{equation}
f(t) \;=\; f_{\text{nom}} + f_{\text{amp}}\sin(\omega_f\,t),
\qquad f \in \{A_s,\,B_s,\,C_s,\,K_s\}.
\label{eq:slosh-coeff}
\end{equation}
Requiring $f_{\text{nom}} > |f_{\text{amp}}|$ keeps $K_s,C_s$ strictly
positive (a negative stiffness or damping would be unphysical). With the
default $f_{\text{amp}} = 0$, each coefficient reduces to a constant equal
to $f_{\text{nom}}$.

Physically, $K_s$ is the slosh mode's spring-like restoring stiffness,
$C_s$ its damping, and $A_s, B_s$ are forcing gains that convert the
spacecraft's own body rate and angular acceleration into a slosh-torque
excitation --- the more aggressively the spacecraft rotates or
accelerates, the harder the propellant sloshes back.

\subsubsection{Model ``testing'': Rate-Dependent Stiffness/Damping Model}
\label{sec:slosh-testing}

An alternate second-order model replaces the constant/sinusoidal
coefficients of Eq.~\eqref{eq:slosh} with stiffness and damping terms that
scale with the \emph{ratio} of the instantaneous body rate $\Omega$ to a
reference maximum rate $\Omega_{\max}$:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
\ddot{\bm{T}}_{\!s}
\;=\;
 -\,\omega_n^{2}\,\bm{T}_{\!s}
 \;-\;
 \frac{2\zeta\,\omega_n}{1+\bigl(\Omega/\Omega_{\max}\bigr)^{2}}\,
 \dot{\bm{T}}_{\!s}
 \;+\;
 \omega_n^{2}\Bigl(1+\bigl(\Omega/\Omega_{\max}\bigr)^{2}\Bigr)\,
 \dot{\Omega},
\;}
\label{eq:slosh-testing}
\end{equation}
applied independently per body axis, with $\Omega \equiv \omega$ and
$\dot\Omega \equiv \dot{\omega}$ evaluated component-wise. The model
parameters are the sloshing mode's natural frequency $\omega_n$, its
damping ratio $\zeta$, and the saturation rate $\Omega_{\max}$; the
implied stiffness and nominal damping follow the standard second-order
relations $K_s = \omega_n^{2}$ and $C_s = 2\zeta\,\omega_n$. The default
parameters used in the simulation are
\begin{equation}
\omega_n = 0.08944~\text{rad/s}, \qquad
\zeta = 0.1286, \qquad
\Omega_{\max} = 0.418879~\text{rad/s}
\;\bigl(=24^\circ/\text{s}\bigr),
\end{equation}
consistent with $C_s = 23\times10^{-3}$ and $K_s = 8\times10^{-3}$ as
reported for the reference sloshing mode.

Unlike the ``bourdelle'' model, here the effective damping and forcing
gain both scale with $1+(\Omega/\Omega_{\max})^{2}$: as the body spin rate
$\Omega$ approaches the reference rate $\Omega_{\max}$, the damping term
on $\dot{\bm{T}}_{\!s}$ \emph{softens} (its coefficient shrinks, since the
factor appears in the denominator) while the forcing from $\dot{\Omega}$
\emph{strengthens} by the same factor --- i.e.\ the slosh mode becomes
progressively more lightly damped and more strongly excited as the
spacecraft spins faster, modelling a propellant response that grows more
violent near the vehicle's operational rate limit.

\subsection{Net Body Torque Decomposition}
\label{sec:u-body-detail}

The quantity $\bm{u}_{\text{body}}$ appearing on the right-hand side of
Euler's equation~\eqref{eq:euler-wheels} is not itself a single physical
actuator output --- it is the superposition of every torque source
introduced in Sections~\ref{sec:wheels}--\ref{sec:slosh}, each of which can
be independently enabled or disabled:
\begin{equation}
\bm{u}_{\text{body}}
\;=\;
\underbrace{\bm{u}_{\text{act}}}_{\text{actuator torque}}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\bm{\tau}_{\text{mag}}}_{\text{magnetic torque}}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\bm{T}_{\!s}}_{\text{slosh disturbance}}.
\label{eq:u-body-full}
\end{equation}

computed, which configuration toggle controls it, and its physical origin.

\paragraph{Three related but distinct torque quantities.} It is worth
explicitly distinguishing three torque quantities that are easy to
conflate:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
  \item $\bm{u}_{\text{cmd}}$ --- the \emph{raw PD command}
        (Eq.~\ref{eq:pd}), computed directly from attitude/rate error,
        before any actuator allocation.
  \item $\bm{u}_{\text{act}} + \bm{\tau}_{\text{mag}}$ --- the
        \emph{control torque} actually delivered to the body by the
        controller and momentum-management subsystems, \emph{excluding}
        the slosh disturbance. This is precisely the quantity exported
        as the $u_x, u_y, u_z$ columns in the MATLAB data files (see
        Section~\ref{sec:matlab-export}) — a deliberate choice so that
        post-processed data cleanly separates ``what the control system
        did'' from ``what the environment/disturbance did.''
  \item $\bm{u}_{\text{body}}$ --- the \emph{full net torque}
        (Eq.~\ref{eq:u-body-full}) actually substituted into Euler's
        equation, including the slosh contribution $\bm{T}_{\!s}$, which
        is exported separately as its own $T_{s,x}, T_{s,y}, T_{s,z}$
        columns.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Torque Signal Chain and Plotted/Exported Quantities}
\label{sec:torque-chain}

The preceding sections introduce several torque-like quantities in
different places; this section collects them into a single signal
chain, in the order the torque actually flows from controller to body,
and states plainly which ones are directly visible in the plots/MATLAB
export and which are internal-only. Confusing these is the single most
common source of misreading the telemetry plots.

\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{4pt}
\item $\bm{u}_{\text{cmd}}$ (plotted as $T_c$) --- the PD controller's
      raw output (Eq.~\ref{eq:pd}): \emph{``what net body torque does the
      controller want?''} Computed directly from attitude/rate error,
      before any allocation or actuator model.
\item $\bm{\tau}_{w,\text{cmd}}$ (\emph{not} separately plotted) ---
      $\bm{u}_{\text{cmd}}$ allocated across the $n_w$ physical wheels
      (Eq.~\ref{eq:wheel-alloc}): \emph{``what should each wheel motor
      do?''}
\item $\bm{\tau}_w$ (plotted as \emph{Wheel torque}) --- the torque each
      wheel \emph{actually delivers}. Equal to $\bm{\tau}_{w,\text{cmd}}$
      when the wheel actuator dynamics of Sec.~\ref{sec:wheel-actuator}
      are disabled (the default); otherwise the output of the low-pass
      filter, Eq.~\ref{eq:wheel-tf}, lagging behind
      $\bm{\tau}_{w,\text{cmd}}$.
\item $\bm{u}_{\text{act}}$ (\emph{not} separately plotted) --- $\bm{\tau}_w$
      converted back from ``per wheel'' to ``body $x,y,z$'' via
      $\bm{u}_{\text{act}} = -\bm{W}\bm{\tau}_w$ (the wheel reaction onto
      the body, Table~\ref{tab:u-body-terms}).
\item $\bm{\tau}_{\text{mag}}$ (plotted as \emph{Mag.\ torque}) --- the
      magnetorquer's momentum-dumping torque, Eq.~\ref{eq:bdot},
      computed independently of the wheel/PD path.
\item $\bm{T}_{\!s}$ (plotted as \emph{Slosh torque}) --- the propellant
      slosh disturbance, an integrated state (Sec.~\ref{sec:slosh}), not
      commanded by anything.
\item $\bm{u}_{\text{body}}$ (\emph{not} separately plotted) --- the sum
      of all three physical contributions, Eq.~\ref{eq:u-body-full}: the
      one true net torque substituted into Euler's equation
      (Eq.~\ref{eq:euler-wheels}) to produce $\dot{\bm{\omega}}$.
\end{enumerate}

Schematically, the chain is
\begin{equation}
\bm{u}_{\text{cmd}}
\;\xrightarrow{\text{allocate, Eq.~\ref{eq:wheel-alloc}}}\;
\bm{\tau}_{w,\text{cmd}}
\;\xrightarrow{\text{actuator filter, Eq.~\ref{eq:wheel-tf}}}\;
\bm{\tau}_w
\;\xrightarrow{-\bm{W}(\cdot)}\;
\bm{u}_{\text{act}}
\;+\;
\bm{\tau}_{\text{mag}}
\;+\;
\bm{T}_{\!s}
\;=\;
\bm{u}_{\text{body}}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\dot{\bm{\omega}}.
\label{eq:torque-chain}
\end{equation}

Table~\ref{tab:torque-visibility} states exactly where each quantity can
be seen (or cannot).

A common point of confusion: enabling the wheel actuator dynamics
(Sec.~\ref{sec:wheel-actuator}) visibly smooths the \emph{Wheel torque}
panel and the exported $u_x,u_y,u_z$ columns (since both are downstream
of $\bm{\tau}_w$) --- but the $T_c$ panel is $\bm{u}_{\text{cmd}}$,
computed \emph{upstream} of the actuator filter, so it always looks
instantaneous regardless of the actuator dynamics toggle. Looking at the
$T_c$ panel to check whether the actuator filter is working is the
single easiest way to draw the wrong conclusion from these plots.


% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{State Vector and Equations of Motion}
\label{sec:state}

Collecting the previous results, the full simulation state is
\begin{equation}
\bm{x}(t) \;=\;
\bigl[\,\bm{q};\; \bm{\omega};\; \bm{h}_w;\; \bm{T}_{\!s};\;
       \dot{\bm{T}}_{\!s}\,\bigr]
\;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{\,13+n_w},
\end{equation}
governed by
\begin{align}
\dot{\bm{q}} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\,\bm{\Omega}(\bm{\omega})\,\bm{q}
   && \text{(kinematics, Eq.~\ref{eq:quat-kin})} \\[2pt]
\bm{J}\,\dot{\bm{\omega}} &= \bm{u}_{\text{body}}
 \;-\; \bm{\omega}\times\!\bigl(\bm{J}\bm{\omega}+\bm{W}\bm{h}_w\bigr)
   && \text{(Euler, Eq.~\ref{eq:euler-wheels})} \\[2pt]
\dot{\bm{h}}_w &= \bm{\tau}_w \;=\; -\bm{W}^{+}\bm{u}_{\text{cmd}}
   && \text{(wheel momentum)} \\[2pt]
\ddot{\bm{T}}_{\!s} &=
   -A_s\bm{\omega} - B_s\dot{\bm{\omega}}
   - C_s\dot{\bm{T}}_{\!s} - K_s\bm{T}_{\!s}
   && \text{(slosh, Eq.~\ref{eq:slosh})}
\end{align}
with
\(
\bm{u}_{\text{body}} \;=\;
-\bm{W}\bm{\tau}_w \;+\; \bm{m}\times\bm{B} \;+\; \bm{T}_{\!s},
\)
$\bm{u}_{\text{cmd}}$ given by~\eqref{eq:pd}, and $\bm{m}$
given by~\eqref{eq:bdot}.


% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Euler-Angle Conversion}
\label{sec:euler-conv}

For initial-condition specification and post-processing visualisation,
3-2-1 ($Z$-$Y$-$X$) yaw--pitch--roll Euler angles $(\phi,\theta,\psi)$
are converted to and from the scalar-last quaternion using
App.~B of~\cite{markley2014}:
\begin{equation}
\bm{q} \;=\; \bm{q}_z(\psi)\otimes\bm{q}_y(\theta)\otimes\bm{q}_x(\phi).
\end{equation}
The inverse extraction, used for the angle/rate output plots, is
\begin{align}
\phi   &= \operatorname{atan2}\!\bigl(2(q_4 q_1 + q_2 q_3),\;
                                      1 - 2(q_1^{2} + q_2^{2})\bigr), \\[2pt]
\theta &= \arcsin\!\bigl(\operatorname{clip}\bigl(2(q_4 q_2 - q_1 q_3),\,
                                                  -1,\,+1\bigr)\bigr), \\[2pt]
\psi   &= \operatorname{atan2}\!\bigl(2(q_4 q_3 + q_1 q_2),\;
                                      1 - 2(q_2^{2} + q_3^{2})\bigr),
\end{align}
where the $\arcsin$ argument is clipped to $[-1,+1]$ for numerical
robustness in the neighbourhood of the gimbal-lock singularity at
$\theta = \pm 90^\circ$.


% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Numerical Integration}
\label{sec:integration}

Two integrators are provided for the augmented state:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
  \item a hand-written fixed-step classical fourth-order Runge--Kutta
        (RK4) with quaternion re-normalisation after each step
        (zero-order hold on controller outputs over each $\Delta t$);
  \item an adaptive solver via SciPy's
        \texttt{scipy.integrate.solve\_ivp}
        (\texttt{RK45} / \texttt{DOP853} / \texttt{LSODA}), with the
        closed-loop treated as a continuous-time system and quaternion
        re-normalisation applied in post-processing.
\end{itemize}
Both integrators share the same dynamics, controller, and slosh
modules; only the time-stepping strategy differs.

\section{Multi-Trajectory Batch Simulations}
\label{sec:batch}

It is often desirable to evaluate a \emph{family} of attitude maneuvers on
one physically fixed spacecraft --- e.g.\ to characterise controller
performance across a range of start/target attitudes --- while holding
every platform property (inertia tensor, wheel geometry, gains, slosh
model and coefficients) constant. The batch driver \texttt{run\_batch()}
implements exactly this pattern.

\subsection{Base Configuration and Scenario Overrides}
\label{sec:batch-config}

A single \emph{base configuration} carries every fixed platform property.
A \emph{scenario} is a small dictionary specifying only the handful of
fields that vary between runs: the initial attitude quaternion
$\bm{q}_0$, the target attitude quaternion $\bm{q}_{\text{des}}$, and
optionally a nonzero initial body rate $\bm{\omega}_0$. For each scenario,
\texttt{run\_batch()}:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
  \item makes an independent \emph{deep copy} of the base configuration
        (essential, since the configuration holds mutable containers ---
        NumPy arrays and the slosh-coefficient dictionary --- that a
        shallow copy would leave \emph{shared} across scenarios, silently
        corrupting earlier or later runs);
  \item overrides only the fields present in that scenario's dictionary
        ($\bm{q}_0$, $\bm{q}_{\text{des}}$, and/or $\bm{\omega}_0$);
  \item runs the full closed-loop simulation of Section~\ref{sec:state}
        unchanged, via \texttt{simulate()};
\end{enumerate}
and returns the list of per-scenario results, in the same order as the
input scenario list. Wheel momentum $\bm{h}_w$ is not an overridable
scenario field; every run begins with the wheels at rest
($\bm{h}_w(0)=\bm{0}$), consistent with Section~\ref{sec:wheels}.

\subsection{Example Scenario Set: Ten Attitude Slews}
\label{sec:batch-example}

A \emph{slew} maneuver is a commanded rotation from one attitude to
another --- the spacecraft is driven from $\bm{q}_0$ to
$\bm{q}_{\text{des}}$ by the closed-loop controller of
Section~\ref{sec:pd-controller}, as opposed to a small stationkeeping
correction. The example script builds ten such slews, all capped at
$45^\circ$ per axis, designed to isolate distinct coupling behaviours
one at a time. Table~\ref{tab:ten-scenarios} lists the set; all start
from $(\phi,\theta,\psi) = (0,0,0)^\circ$ unless noted.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Ten example attitude-slew scenarios (3-2-1 Euler angles in
degrees; body rates in rad/s).}
\label{tab:ten-scenarios}
\begin{tabular}{@{}clp{7.2cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{\#} & \textbf{Target $(\phi,\theta,\psi)$} & \textbf{Purpose} \\
\midrule
1  & $(45,0,0)$      & Roll-only --- isolates $x$-axis response. \\
2  & $(0,45,0)$      & Pitch-only --- isolates $y$-axis response. \\
3  & $(0,0,45)$      & Yaw-only --- isolates $z$-axis response. \\
4  & $(45,45,0)$     & Roll+pitch coupled. \\
5  & $(45,0,45)$     & Roll+yaw coupled. \\
6  & $(0,45,45)$     & Pitch+yaw coupled. \\
7  & $(45,45,45)$    & All three axes simultaneously (maximum coupling). \\
8  & $(-45,-45,-45)$ & Same magnitude as \#7, opposite direction ---
                        checks symmetry of the response. \\

                        coupling behaves consistently when commanded
                        directions differ, not only when all axes share
                        the same sign. \\
10 & $(10,10,10)$    & Small-angle target reached from a \emph{nonzero}

\subsection{Batch Plotting}
\label{sec:batch-plot}

For each scenario, two figure windows are produced: the full
nine-panel telemetry figure (attitude quaternion, error quaternion, body
rate, control torque, wheel momentum/torque, magnetorquer dipole/torque,
and slosh torque) and a compact two-panel Euler-angle/body-rate figure,
each titled with the scenario's start and target angles. Figures may
optionally be written to disk as PNG images in a specified output
directory, in addition to being displayed interactively.

\section{MATLAB Data Export (.mat)}
\label{sec:matlab-export}

Simulation results are exported to MATLAB-compatible \texttt{.mat} files
for post-processing outside the Python environment. Two related export
functions share a common per-trajectory column layout: one exports a
single trajectory, the other stacks an entire batch (Section~\ref{sec:batch})
into one file.

\subsection{Per-Trajectory Column Layout}
\label{sec:matlab-columns}

Every exported trajectory is reduced to a single $(n_t \times 19)$
matrix: one time column followed by eighteen data columns, grouped into
six physical quantities of three components each. Body angular
acceleration $\dot{\bm{\omega}}$ is not itself an integrated state (only
$\bm{\omega}$ is), so it is reconstructed by numerically differentiating
the saved $\bm{\omega}$ history; likewise the Euler angles are obtained
from the quaternion history via the conversion of
Section~\ref{sec:euler-conv}, purely for human-readable output. Table~
\ref{tab:matlab-columns} lists every column.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Column layout of the exported $(n_t\times 19)$ data matrix.}
\label{tab:matlab-columns}
\begin{tabular}{@{}clcp{6.4cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Col.} & \textbf{Name(s)} & \textbf{Units} & \textbf{Description} \\
\midrule
1     & \texttt{t} & s & Simulation time. \\
2--4  & \texttt{roll, pitch, yaw} & deg &
        3-2-1 Euler angles $(\phi,\theta,\psi)$, Section~\ref{sec:euler-conv}. \\
5--7  & \texttt{wx, wy, wz} & rad/s &
        Body angular velocity $\bm{\omega}$. \\
8--10 & \texttt{wx\_dot, wy\_dot, wz\_dot} & rad/s$^2$ &
        Body angular acceleration $\dot{\bm{\omega}}$, obtained by
        numerical differentiation of $\bm{\omega}$ (not directly
        integrated by the solver). \\
11--13 & \texttt{ux, uy, uz} & N$\cdot$m &
        \emph{Control torque}: $\bm{u}_{\text{act}}+\bm{\tau}_{\text{mag}}$
        --- the actuator plus magnetorquer torque actually applied to the
        body, deliberately \emph{excluding} the slosh disturbance
        $\bm{T}_{\!s}$ (see Section~\ref{sec:u-body-detail}). \\
14--16 & \texttt{Tsx, Tsy, Tsz} & N$\cdot$m &
        Propellant slosh disturbance torque $\bm{T}_{\!s}$
        (Section~\ref{sec:slosh}); identically zero when
        \texttt{enable\_slosh} is off. \\
17--19 & \texttt{Fsx, Fsy, Fsz} & N &
        \emph{Placeholder} sloshing \emph{force} columns, currently held
        at exactly zero. Reserved for a future force-level slosh model;
        not yet physically modelled. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsection{Single-Trajectory File}
\label{sec:matlab-single}

For one simulation run, the exported \texttt{.mat} file contains two
top-level variables:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
  \item \texttt{data} --- the $(n_t\times 19)$ matrix of
        Table~\ref{tab:matlab-columns};
  \item \texttt{labels} --- a $1\times 19$ cell array of column-name
        strings, in the same order as \texttt{data}'s columns.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Batch File}
\label{sec:matlab-batch}

For a batch of $N$ scenarios (Section~\ref{sec:batch}), all trajectories
are stacked into a single \texttt{.mat} file containing one top-level
variable:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
  \item \texttt{trajectories} --- a $1\times N$ MATLAB \emph{cell array},
        one cell per scenario, each holding a \emph{struct} with fields:
    \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
      \item \texttt{data} --- that scenario's $(n_t\times 19)$ matrix
            (Table~\ref{tab:matlab-columns});
      \item \texttt{labels} --- the same $1\times 19$ column-name cell
            array;
      \item \texttt{q0\_deg} --- $[\,\phi_0,\theta_0,\psi_0\,]$, the
            scenario's initial Euler angles, in degrees;
      \item \texttt{qdes\_deg} --- $[\,\phi_{\text{des}},
            \theta_{\text{des}},\psi_{\text{des}}\,]$, the scenario's
            target Euler angles, in degrees.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
In MATLAB, an individual trajectory's data is accessed as
\texttt{trajectories\{i\}.data}, with column names available via
\texttt{trajectories\{i\}.labels}.

\paragraph{Gimbal-lock note on \texttt{q0\_deg}/\texttt{qdes\_deg}.} These
degree labels are recorded from the \emph{exact} angles originally
requested when the scenario was constructed, rather than recovered after
the fact from the quaternion via the inverse conversion of
Section~\ref{sec:euler-conv}. The two are not always equivalent: at pitch
$=\pm 90^\circ$ (gimbal lock), infinitely many $(\phi,\theta,\psi)$
triples map to the same physical attitude quaternion, so recovering
angles by inverse conversion can report a triple that is
mathematically equivalent but visually different from what was actually
requested (e.g.\ $(180^\circ,90^\circ,180^\circ)$ instead of the intended
$(0^\circ,90^\circ,0^\circ)$). Recording the original request avoids this
ambiguity; the underlying simulated dynamics are unaffected either way,
since the simulation operates on quaternions throughout and only these
two label fields are affected.
